Taglish dictation for Mac: what good voice typing should handle.

Taglish is not broken English or simplified Tagalog. It is natural code-switching, and a useful dictation tool has to follow both languages inside the same thought.

Updated 29 July 2026 · 6 minute read

Why Taglish is difficult for speech-to-text

A Taglish sentence can move from Filipino grammar to English nouns, workplace terms, and product names without warning. The speaker does not pause to announce the switch. A speech model has to decide which language it is hearing while it is still interpreting the sentence.

That creates several common failure points: an English word may be forced into a Filipino-sounding spelling, a Filipino affix may be separated from an English root, or punctuation may disappear when the language changes.

“Pwede mo ba i-double check yung invoice? Baka may mali sa total.”

A useful test is not whether the app understands isolated Tagalog words. It is whether it keeps phrases such as “i-double check”, “leave request ko”, and “send mo na lang” intact inside real sentences.

How to test a Taglish dictation app

Use your own voice and your own work vocabulary. A polished product demo tells you what the developer chose to show; a personal test tells you whether the app understands you.

  • Speak at your normal speed instead of slowing down for the software.
  • Mix Filipino and English inside one sentence, not in separate recordings.
  • Include names, acronyms, dates, and the tools you use every day.
  • Try a quiet room and a typical noisy environment.
  • Count missing or changed words before editing the result.

Three sentences worth trying

“Na-approve na ba yung leave request ko for next week?”

“Send mo na lang sa akin yung report bago mag-lunch, please.”

“I-update natin yung client after ng stand-up para clear yung next steps.”

What accuracy should you expect?

No dictation app is perfect, especially with names, uncommon jargon, background noise, and similar-sounding words. Judge the result by how much correction your normal work requires—not by a single percentage without a published test method.

Bulong publishes raw Taglish examples on its homepage, including a real “mag-lunch” to “mag-launch” error. That miss matters because honest results make it easier to decide whether the correction burden is acceptable.

A practical standard

If speaking plus correcting is consistently faster and more comfortable than typing, the tool is doing useful work. If every sentence needs repair, headline accuracy numbers do not matter.

Privacy matters more when you speak naturally

People dictate messages, client notes, personal reminders, and unfinished thoughts. Those are often more sensitive than the text they deliberately paste into an online service.

With on-device dictation, the speech model runs on the Mac. After its one-time model download, Bulong does not upload audio or transcripts for processing. You can verify that behavior by turning off Wi-Fi and dictating again.

Read the full Bulong privacy explanation or the offline dictation guide before deciding which processing model fits your work.

The short version

Good Taglish voice typing should understand code-switching without making you separate Filipino and English into different recordings. Test the app with your actual accent, microphone, vocabulary, and sentence structure. Keep the raw result visible, and choose the tool that needs the least correction while meeting your privacy requirements.
